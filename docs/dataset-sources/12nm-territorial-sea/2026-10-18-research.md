## Authority, distribution and lineage

- [OCS landing page](https://www.nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/data/us-maritime-limits-and-boundaries.html)
  explicitly links the exact REST service, SHP ZIP, KMZ and InPort metadata.
- [NOAA InPort 39963](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/39963)
  identifies OCS as originator/distributor and documents nautical-chart-derived
  baselines reviewed by the interagency U.S. Baseline Committee. The parent
  also includes international boundaries with separate International Boundary
  Commission lineage; that is not a claim that each territorial-sea segment
  originated with that commission.
- [REST service](https://maritimeboundaries.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/MaritimeBoundaries/US_Maritime_Limits_Boundaries/MapServer?f=pjson)
  credits NOAA/NOS/OCS and lists the exact title as layer 1.
  This is self-hosted ArcGIS Server. An ArcGIS Online publishing account is
  not exposed. No account name or delegated distributor is inferred.
- [NOAA dictionary 41512](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/41512)
  describes `BOUND_ID` as the boundary-segment identifier and FID/OBJECTID as
  an automatically generated internal number. The dictionary describes some
  static-format fields and omits service `RULEID`; it is not the exact REST
  schema. The captured layer metadata is the actual schema observation.

The inventory's exact filename/title, description, NOAA agency and DataLumos
238490 pointer agree with issue #2. However, the original
[DHS crosswalk workbook](https://doi.org/10.3886/E241367V1) could not be obtained:
the DOI and direct DataLumos 241367 requests failed at proxy CONNECT with 403,
and the web tool also could not fetch the project. **Crosswalk row matching
and original hyperlink targets are unverified**, not an accepted title match
or an assertion that no row exists.

## Anonymous extraction and service health

Machine URL:
https://maritimeboundaries.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/MaritimeBoundaries/US_Maritime_Limits_Boundaries/MapServer/1/query?where=1%3D1&outFields=*&f=geojson

All source calls used ordinary verified HTTPS through the inherited proxy,
without agency authentication. `evidence/requests.json` records request UTC
times, full public URLs, final URLs, HTTP status, body size and SHA-256; it
also records parsed ArcGIS errors. The ten successful historical response bodies required for the audit are now
preserved losslessly in `evidence/historical-captures-20261019.tar.gz`; static
format downloads and other scratch responses remain outside the PR. `evidence/sample.geojson` contains one complete, small feature
as an illustrative sample, not the completeness proof.

The count endpoint returned **87**, the ID endpoint returned **87 distinct
OBJECTIDs**, and the GeoJSON response contained **87** features with exactly
the same OBJECTID set. GeoJSON feature `id` equals property `OBJECTID` for
every feature. The advertised maximum is 1,000 records and pagination is
supported. Ordered 25-record JSON requests at offsets 0, 25 and 75 returned
25, 25 and 12 records. Offset 50 failed twice; requesting the corresponding
25 explicit OBJECTIDs recovered those records. The combined 87 JSON records
have no duplicate/missing IDs and equal **every** GeoJSON attribute and
coordinate, matched by OBJECTID. The first two pages report transfer-limit
continuation; the explicit-ID chunk and last page do not. An offset-100 retry
returned an empty page, as expected.

Several requests, including initial layer metadata, page 50 and final ID
rechecks, returned HTTP 200 containing **ArcGIS error 500** saying the service
could not be found or might be stopped. These were rejected, never counted as
zero rows or successful metadata. A later metadata response succeeded and was
byte-identical to the NOAA `encdirect.noaa.gov` endpoint inspected here. That
second hostname is corroboration, not an unverified replacement source.

Counts remained 87 before/after extraction; successful ID responses at 03:47
and 03:48 UTC were identical. The final 03:49 and 03:50 ID requests failed.
Therefore the audit proves completeness for the observed ID set and agreement
of the retrieved records, **not an atomic or immutable producer snapshot**.
Future implementations must retry within bounds, reconcile complete IDs,
detect changes during extraction, and quarantine drift rather than silently
publish a partial response. No credentials are a remedy for these anonymous
service errors established by this audit.

## Schema, identifiers, geometry and coverage

The service exposes 12 attribute fields plus `SHAPE` geometry:
`OBJECTID`, `BOUND_ID`, `REGION`, `FEAT_TYPE`, `PUB_DATE`, `APPRV_DATE`,
`LEGAL_AUTH`, `AOR`, `NOTE`, `SUPP_INFO`, `UNILATERAL`, `RULEID`.
Full Esri types, aliases, string lengths and RULEID domain are retained in
`evidence/noaa-layer.json`; observed JSON types, distinct counts and nulls
are in `evidence/extraction-audit.json`. Producer nullability constraints are
not exposed and remain unknown. `PUB_DATE` has 9 nulls; `NOTE` has 17 nulls
and also single-space values. `SUPP_INFO` includes padded strings. Preserve
those source values rather than trimming or collapsing them during research.

`OBJECTID` and five-character `BOUND_ID` are both non-null and unique across
all 87 features. Preserve both, including the zeros in identifiers such as
`B0133`. This retrieval does not establish cross-release OBJECTID stability
or continuity of BOUND_ID across splits/merges.

Geometry is **polyline**, EPSG:4326/WGS84, without Z or M. GeoJSON contains
80 LineStrings and 7 MultiLineStrings; Shapely found zero null, empty or
invalid geometries. These are outer limit **lines**, not territorial-sea
area polygons. Observed extent is
`[-180, -14.760835789999987, 180, 71.588953446]`; the advertised layer extent
is broader (`[-180, -17.555000002, 180, 74.70884000400001]`). Do not mistake
the parent/service extent for observed layer coverage.

All 11 observed regions and their counts are recorded in the audit: Alaska
29; Atlantic Coast and Gulf of Mexico 15; Hawaiian Islands 13; CNMI/Guam 11;
American Samoa 4; Puerto Rico/U.S. Virgin Islands 4; Pacific Coast 4;
Howland/Baker/Jarvis 3; Palmyra/Kingman 2; Wake 1; Johnston 1. These checks
cover the retrieved layer, not an independent proof of complete legal
territorial coverage.

## Dates and maintained-source uncertainty

The service advertises **version 4.1, revised 2013-09-13**. Per-segment
`PUB_DATE` ranges from **2003-08-27 to 2013-09-13**, with 9 unknown dates;
approval dates range from **2003-04-02 to 2013-09-10**. Dates are Unix
milliseconds in the response. A record approval date is not a release date.

InPort describes ongoing/continual maintenance but still reports publication
and revision year 2013, extent 2002–2015, and an update forthcoming. Its
**2026-08-20 metadata edit is not a data release**. The landing page describes
event-driven revisions, sometimes every few months, without establishing a
newer release for these bytes. The inventory's 2024-04-30 issued and
2025-04-08 modified dates likewise do not supersede the producer evidence.
The latest maintained agency release remains unverified. No assertion that
this is current 2026 data or that NOAA has abandoned the source is justified.

## Other formats and preservation copies

The landing-page-linked official
[SHP ZIP](https://maritimeboundaries.noaa.gov/downloads/USMaritimeLimitsAndBoundariesSHP.zip)
and [KMZ](https://maritimeboundaries.noaa.gov/downloads/USMaritimeLimitsAndBoundariesKML.kmz)
were downloaded and their ZIP structures inspected. The SHP has 260 records
across maritime limits, maritime boundaries and land boundaries. Its `TS=1`
subset has 110 records. All 87 named-layer BOUND_IDs and OBJECTIDs occur in
the parent, but the extra 23 TS records mean `TS=1` is **not** an equivalent
replacement filter. The SHP has `TS`, `CZ`, `EEZ`, `F_EEZ`, `SYMBOL` instead
of service `RULEID`. See `evidence/static-formats.json`. The KMZ was ZIP
integrity-checked but its layer count/equivalence was not verified. March 2020
ZIP timestamps describe packaging, not a new producer data release.

The web-rendered [DataLumos V1 archive](https://www.datalumos.org/datalumos/project/238490/version/V1/view)
lists NOAA/DHS attribution, ICPSR distribution and archive publication
2025-09-30, with GeoJSON ZIP, shapefile ZIP and metadata XML. Its own
description identifies preservation from deactivated HIFLD Open. This is a
historical archive, not a maintained NOAA feed; direct bytes were blocked
here. PASDA, SeerAI/Source Cooperative and Esri archive copies are distinct
preservation candidates from prior issue #40, not audited item-specific
fallbacks or proven delegated official distributions in this pilot.

## Published baseline and proposed contract

The [HIFLD Next schema page](https://hifld.publicenvirodata.org/collections/hifld/datasets/12nm-territorial-sea/files/12nm-territorial-sea/schema)

`12nm-territorial-sea`, **v1.0.0**, 87 features and 14 columns, including a
separate published `id` column and MultiLineString geometry. The proposed
contract in `source.json` records **every** column's reported type,
nullability, meaning and ID role, without claiming approval or exact physical
Parquet types. Portal metadata is not a full accepted-data comparison.

Pinned candidate asset URLs, catalog checksum strings and release catalog ID
are in `source.json`. Direct requests to the versioned baseline GeoJSON and
portal metadata were blocked by proxy CONNECT 403; no access expansion was
attempted. Baseline checksums were not recomputed and full baseline rows
were not obtained. Matching counts, dates and extent alone cannot establish
exact continuation. Do not silently drop the existing published `id`,
change epoch-date types, coerce service double `UNILATERAL` to integer, or
normalize mixed geometries without an approved mapping. No new row IDs were
created.

Implementation still requires issue #417's shared comparison/promotion
prerequisites, a maintainer-approved pinned baseline and raw/published schema
and identifier contracts. Missing baseline evidence and possible format/
